The experiment

Same topic. Two very different requests.

Keep both Copilot conversations available for comparison.

Prompt AAsk for an answer
Why does Earth have seasons?
Notice:
  • Is it easy to understand?
  • Does it help you remember?
  • Does it make you think?
  • What might need verifying?
Prompt BAsk for a tutor
I am a 15-year-old student. Help me learn why Earth has seasons. Start by asking me what I already know. Then explain it in simple language using an everyday analogy. Do not give me more than 120 words at a time. Finish by asking me one question and wait for my answer.
Then:
  • Answer Copilot’s question.
  • Continue for at least one reply.
  • Notice who is doing the thinking.

Compare in pairs

Which version produced more learning?

  1. Which response made you think more?
  2. What did prompt B do differently?
  3. Would you trust either answer without checking?
✓

Verification checkpointThe seasons are mainly caused by Earth’s axial tilt — not by Earth moving closer to or farther from the Sun.

The anatomy of the prompt

Five instructions create the experience.

A long prompt is not automatically a good prompt. Each instruction needs a purpose.

01

Goal

Define exactly what the learner needs to understand or practise.

02

Context

Give the subject, topic, age and current level.

03

Format

Ask for four short scenes and one question at a time.

04

Rules

Control hints, health points, answers and length.

05

Interaction

Make Copilot wait and react to the learner’s choices.

03 · Listen critically

Audio overview

Evidence detective mission

Complete the four lab stations.

You are evaluating the study kit, not testing your ability to create an account.

01

Extract

Write down one claim that appears in at least two resources. Record which two formats included it.

02

Qualify

Find one phrase showing uncertainty or limited evidence. Explain why cautious wording matters more than a confident tone.

03

Test

Choose one claim from the infographic. Check whether the evidence deck and audio repeat, qualify or contradict it. Decide whether the claim looks well supported, uncertain or exaggerated.

04

Challenge

Ask Copilot for a short answer without giving it the teacher’s source set. Compare its answer with the source-based materials.

Without searching the web, answer in 120 words: Has TikTok destroyed teenagers’ attention spans? Separate what is well supported, uncertain and possibly exaggerated. Do not invent sources.

Tool showdown

Which output helped, and which one only looked impressive?

  1. Which format made the topic easiest to understand?
  2. Which format would help you remember it tomorrow?
  3. What important detail disappeared when the information became visual or audio?
  4. Was Copilot more certain than the source-based materials?
✓

The ruleA polished output is not evidence that it is accurate. Trace important claims back to the original source.

04 One topic. Two tools. Two prompts.

Design better prompts.
Build better games.

Use Copilot to create a tool-specific instruction for Wordwall and another for Educaplay, then test whether the resulting games genuinely support learning.

0%lesson complete
A good prompt fits the tool, the learner and the learning goal.

Prompt showdown

Why is “Make me a game about volcanoes” not enough?

With a partner, identify three details the AI still needs. Think about the learner, the learning goal, the game format and the level of challenge.

Teacher demonstration · first 5 minutes

The same topic needs different instructions.

  1. Turn a vague request into a precise Wordwall content prompt.
  2. Turn the same choices into an instruction for Educaplay’s Ray.
  3. Notice which details stay the same and which change with the tool.

Today’s rule: Copilot helps you design. You still choose the learning goal, check the content and repair weak questions.

Steps 2–3 · Creation lab

Use each prompt for a different job.

Work in pairs. One game should support rapid recall; the other should require a different kind of thinking.

Tool AReady-to-enter content

Wordwall Sprint

  1. Copy Prompt A into Copilot.
  2. Check its facts, answers and distractors.
  3. Open Wordwall and choose the format selected above.
  4. Enter Copilot’s content manually, publish and copy the player link.

Why this route? You are using Copilot to prepare structured content that works even if Wordwall’s own AI generator is unavailable.

Open Wordwall ↗
Tool BA prompt for another AI

Educaplay Challenge

  1. Copy Prompt B into Copilot.
  2. Copy only the final Ray instruction that Copilot returns.
  3. Paste that instruction into Ray and generate the chosen activity.
  4. Review, edit, publish and copy the player link.

Notice the difference: Copilot is not creating the activity content directly. It is writing a stronger instruction for Ray.

Open Educaplay ↗

Step 4 · Human quality control

Do not publish the first AI draft.

Inspect both activities and make at least one meaningful improvement.

✓Accuracy

Check any fact or answer that you are not certain about.

?Clarity

There is only one defensible answer unless several are intended.

↗Challenge

The wrong answers are plausible, not ridiculous.

◎Learning

The game matches the learning goal instead of testing trivia.

Step 5 · Play, compare and repair

Swap links. Play without help.

  1. Could you open both links?
  2. Which game was clearer?
  3. Which required more thinking?
  4. Did you find an error or ambiguous answer?
  5. Did each game match its learning goal?
✓

Required repairImprove at least one question, answer, instruction or distractor after receiving feedback. A colour change does not count.

Step 6 · Publish and record

Prove that both games work.

01

Publish

Use a title without your full name or personal information.

02

Copy

Copy the player link — never an editing link.

03

Test

Open each link in a private or InPrivate window.

04

Record

Add both prompts, both links and your repair to the portfolio.

Privacy check: Activities may be public. Do not include surnames, photographs, school email addresses or personal information.
